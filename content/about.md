## So, Who Am I?

Hi! My name is **Steven Edward R.**, or just **Drawde**, whatever works. 😅

I'm a self-taught developer, 29 years old as of this writing, and a **Cancer (July)**, if you're into zodiac signs and all that.

I'm not particularly interesting, honestly. This website is really just something I made for fun. There's probably very little here that would genuinely interest you, but since you're already here, you might as well stay for a bit. Make yourself comfortable!

I like developing things, experimenting with technology, making random stuff, and occasionally getting myself into projects that are probably way more complicated than they need to be.

Outside of development, I like art, particularly **Japanese art**, traditional paintings, botanical illustrations, and other things with that lovely old-world aesthetic. I'm also quite fond of **pixel art**. There's something about creating something recognizable with such a limited number of pixels that I find really satisfying.

I guess I just appreciate making things, whether that's through code, pixels, or a paintbrush.

## A Developer of Sorts

I've been teaching myself programming and development since I was around 16. No formal degree in software engineering or computer science, no traditional academic background in the field. Just curiosity, a computer, a lot of trial and error, and probably an unreasonable amount of time spent figuring out why something wasn't working.

I don't really call myself a frontend or backend developer. Full-stack, perhaps? 🤣

I work across different areas depending on what I'm building or experimenting with. I enjoy getting my hands into different parts of a project rather than restricting myself to one particular discipline.

Some of the things I spend my time on include:

### 💻 Web Development

Websites, web applications, interfaces, and the systems behind them. I'm comfortable working with the usual building blocks of the web:

- **HTML** for structure and content.
- **CSS** for styling, layouts, and making things look the way I want.
- **JavaScript** for functionality, interactions, and application logic.
- Backend development, APIs, integrations, and the general business logic that makes applications work.

I'm particularly fond of building things from scratch. Frameworks and templates are useful, but I enjoy having control over how things are structured and implemented.

### 🤖 Artificial Intelligence

This is another rabbit hole I've fallen into, and there's apparently no shortage of things to explore.

My interests include:

- AI model integration and API implementations.
- Custom AI-powered applications and tools.
- Agentic workflows and AI agents.
- Workflow automation and intelligent process orchestration.
- Model fine-tuning for specific use cases.
- Data preparation and curation for model training.

I'm particularly interested in making AI useful beyond simply having a chatbot answer questions. I like exploring how models can be integrated into actual systems, automate processes, interact with tools, and handle more specialized tasks.

### ⚙️ Automation & Systems

I also enjoy building automations, whether through existing automation platforms or custom implementations.

Sometimes a pre-built solution is enough. Other times, I would rather build something myself because the available tools don't quite do what I need.

I like connecting different services, designing workflows, integrating APIs, and making separate components work together as one system.

### 🧠 Data & Model Engineering

Now, this is where things get particularly interesting — and occasionally painful.

I've also spent time exploring data curation and feature engineering for AI model training and fine-tuning.

This is probably one of the most challenging areas I've ventured into. There's a lot more to preparing data for a model than simply collecting a bunch of text and throwing it into a training pipeline.

Data quality, consistency, structure, feature representation, and suitability for a particular training objective all matter.

It's a completely different kind of engineering challenge, and I'm still learning my way through it.

> Apparently, I enjoy making my life difficult by picking up increasingly complicated hobbies. 😖

I don't have one fixed technology stack that I use for absolutely everything. My choices depend on the project, its requirements, and what makes the most sense for the job.

Sometimes the simplest solution is the best one. Sometimes I overcomplicate things. It happens.

## About This Website

Now, before you laugh at the website's design, **this was deliberate, okay?!** 😤

I've made plenty of visually elaborate designs before. Futuristic interfaces, aesthetically polished layouts, animations, fancy transitions, and all that stuff.

I know how to make things look pretty. I promise. 😂

But for this particular website, I wanted something simple. Something that feels like an old desktop environment, with a little bit of nostalgia and a personality of its own.

Nothing too serious, nothing too polished, and definitely nothing that looks like another corporate portfolio.

I actually like the low-effort, slightly old-fashioned look. It's comfortable, familiar, and gives me room to put whatever I want here without worrying too much about maintaining some elaborate visual identity.

Underneath, it's essentially a single HTML file handling the desktop experience, with JavaScript doing the heavy lifting and Markdown files keeping the content separate. It's a fairly simple setup, but it does exactly what I need.

This entire website is just a little personal space on the internet. A place for random thoughts, artwork, experiments, projects, and whatever else I feel like putting up.

No grand plans. No particular audience. No pressure to turn it into something bigger.

Just something I wanted to make.
