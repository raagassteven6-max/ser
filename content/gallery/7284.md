# Kissako

---

A tea bowl. A bamboo scoop. A few confident strokes of ink.

This piece brings together two familiar objects from Japanese tea culture: a **chawan**, the bowl used for tea, and a **chashaku**, the bamboo scoop used to measure matcha. Beside them is the calligraphy **“Kissako”**, commonly translated as *“Have a Cup of Tea.”*

It's a wonderfully simple phrase, but it carries a little more weight than an ordinary invitation.

## A Small Gesture, A Zen Saying

*Kissako* is a well-known Zen expression traced to the teachings of the Chinese master **Zhaozhou (778–897)**. In Japanese tea culture, it came to be understood as a straightforward invitation: come, sit down, and have a cup of tea.

I like how unassuming the subject is. There is no elaborate scene here, just the things needed for tea and a short phrase. The bowl is suggested with broad, layered brushstrokes, while the scoop curves across the lower part of the composition. The calligraphy stands to the side, giving the objects room to breathe.

It's a quiet reminder that sometimes a simple, everyday gesture is enough.

## The Work

- **Period:** 20th century, Shōwa period
- **Medium:** Ink on traditional Japanese shikishi board
- **Subject:** Tea bowl (*chawan*), bamboo tea scoop (*chashaku*), and the Zen inscription *Kissako*
- **Collection:** Daitoku-ji Collection, Kyoto
- **Approximate size:** 24 × 27 cm
- **Detail:** A red seal appears near the lower portion of the board

The work is associated with **Daitoku-ji in Kyoto**, a historic center of Japanese Zen Buddhism and tea culture. The red seal near the bottom adds a small but vivid accent to the otherwise restrained composition.
