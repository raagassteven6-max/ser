# Old Pine Tree and White Phoenix

*老松白鳳図 — Rōshō Hakuhō-zu*

**Itō Jakuchū · c. 1765–1766**
:::

---

A white phoenix poised among dark pine branches, its extraordinary plumage rendered in layer upon layer of fine detail. Around it, the pine needles spread like clouds, while the bird's long, curling tail fills the upper part of the composition with an almost fantastical rhythm.

This is **_Old Pine Tree and White Phoenix_** (*Rōshō Hakuhō-zu*), by **Itō Jakuchū (1716–1800)**, a Japanese painter of the Edo period. It is part of his celebrated series *The Colorful Realm of Living Beings* (*Dōshoku sai-e*).

The painting dates to around **1765–1766**. It is a hanging scroll, made with color on silk, and measures approximately **141.8 × 79.7 cm**. The work is held by the **Museum of the Imperial Collections, Sannomaru Shōzōkan**, in Tokyo, where it is designated a National Treasure.

## A Phoenix Among the Pines

Jakuchū gives the phoenix an almost otherworldly presence without losing the careful observation that makes his bird paintings so remarkable. Its white feathers are built up through intricate, repeated strokes, set against the deep greens and dark textures of the old pine. Small red and green heart-shaped details along the sweeping tail make the whole composition even more distinctive.

The contrast is what I find especially beautiful: the pale, elaborate bird against the dense, almost tangled pine branches. There is so much detail to look at, but the composition still feels balanced.

## A Familiar Image

I have come across this painting a few times over the years, but whenever I see it, my mind goes back to one very specific memory.

Back in fourth grade, our classroom had a calendar with this image on it. I remember it being such a beautiful calendar, and I believe this particular picture was embroidered on it. I can't remember where our teacher got it, or what happened to the calendar afterward, but that image stayed with me.

Ever since then, whenever I happen to see this phoenix somewhere on the internet or in another collection, I immediately think of that classroom and that calendar.

It's funny how that works. I may not remember much about the calendar itself, but I remember this picture.

And apparently, that's enough for it to stick around in my memory all these years. 😅
